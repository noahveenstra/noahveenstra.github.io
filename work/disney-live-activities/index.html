Disney World and Live Activities

Concept · Product design · 2022

Overview

At WWDC 2022, Apple announced Live Activities: a notice that stays on the lock screen and updates in place. You do not unlock, open an app, and hunt for a status.

My Disney Experience already handles mobile order, Lightning Lane, wait times, and maps. Every check still means unlock, open, and navigate. In a park, with several moving pieces and several people, that friction adds up. Live Activities were an obvious fit.

Mobile order

Mobile order was meant to let you place food from anywhere and pick it up when it is ready. After you order, you still keep opening the app to see if it is done, and to remember whether you already said you were there.

A Live Activity would carry three states: confirmed, in preparation, and ready for pickup with the location named. It expires after 20 minutes or when you dismiss it. Tap to expand the full order.

GPS was ruled out. The signal inside buildings is unreliable, and a miss when you are standing at the window is worse than no system.

Lightning Lane

You have a window, you walk to the attraction, and then you dig out a phone, unlock it, find the app, and show someone a screen. Small, and it adds up across a day with a family.

After you reserve, a Live Activity tracks the window. At the attraction, check-in is NFC. The phone is the ticket, the same idea as park entry. These do not need GPS. The attraction and the time window already say when and where.

Turning it off

A new notification behavior can feel invasive. A first-run screen would show what the Live Activity does, what it shows, and how to turn it off. One tap to opt out. The people who want it can see why it helps before they commit.
