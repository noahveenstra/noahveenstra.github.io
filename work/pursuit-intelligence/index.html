    A local library for and work

    Role: concept, data model, ingestion, retrieval, rules

    Scope: 1,334 · · national IT build-out in progress

    This system changed how research gets done: find the story, read the bounded context, reopen the , then decide whether the claim is safe to reuse.

    I designed and built a private, knowledge system that turned more than a thousand records — plus people, projects, history, and documents — into a searchable, evidence-traceable decision library. The proof of concept was strong enough that IT is now building a national version.

    Marketing professional. I owned the problem, the information architecture, the ingestion, the retrieval design, the quality rules, and the operating loop. I did not hand a brief to engineering and wait. I built the working system on a local machine and used it on live marketing, , , and work.

    Public note: This write-up stays generic. It does not name clients or , quote language, or publish pricing, schedules, personnel details, credentials, or unresolved internal records.

    If you are not in

    Construction firms do not usually sell from a catalog. They compete for individual jobs. An (the client) issues a or a . The contractor’s marketing team writes a response that argues: we have done this kind of work, with this kind of team, in this kind of constraint. The that actually lands is not a slogan. It is a — a specific account of what happened on a real job.

    That is the industry context for this system.

    The problem

    In marketing, the gold is the . What the company actually did in the field. How it worked with the . How it worked with the . How it coordinated other . Those stories carry a . Capability slides do not.

    The firm had been writing those stories for years. It had not cataloged them. There was no reliable place to ask a specific question and get a specific, sourced answer. The shared catalog that existed was incomplete and never sharp enough from to .

    The real workflow was a phone call. Marketing called the or , hoped they picked up, and often got pointed to another someone had already been told to write. That was slow, uneven, and easy to treat as fact when it was only memory.

    The hidden risk was worse than slowness. Once a team starts “searching” old files with AI, it becomes easy to repeat an unsupported claim: a person who merely appeared in a becomes “the on that job,” a becomes a , a shared project becomes proven collaboration, a win label becomes a causal theory. I needed something that made work faster without making bad claims easier to recycle.

    What I built

    I built a private, and retrieval system — markdown material, structured records, and a relationship layer — organized around a simple loop:

    SEARCH → READ → VERIFY

    Search returns a small, decision-ready shortlist.

    Reading opens bounded context.

    Verification reopens the governing before a fact, metric, role, story, or commitment is reused.

    This was not a multi-user product. It was a working library on a local computer, used for marketing, , , and work. When someone outside marketing needed an ultra-specific story, they came to the person who had the system.

    The system is organized around four zones:

    Intake

    New material sits here until it is classified and checked.

    Converted material and locators so a claim can be reopened.

    Reusable knowledge

    Controlled people, project, story, strategy, relationship, and response records.

    work

    Compact intelligence and material; live production stays in the workspace.

    It is not a task tracker. It is not the live file. It is not permission to treat a search hit as client-ready copy. It is a decision library with boundaries.

    Current scale

    • 1,334 records
    • 8,705 canonical project views
    • 317 controlled people profiles
    • 9 (on purpose — a story is a reviewed object, not every project page)
    • 137 strategy records

    Those numbers matter less than the distinction they encode. The system can hold thousands of project views and still refuse to call something an until it earns that status.

    What made it more than a search box

    A search box answers “which files contain these words.” That is not the job. The job is “can I put this claim in front of an on Thursday.” The eight design choices below are how the system stays useful without becoming a rumor engine.

    1. A usable front door

    The old experience was a file tree. You remembered a project nickname, guessed a year, opened a PDF, scrolled, and hoped the story you needed was in that version and not the one from two later. If you did not already know where to look, you called the field.

    The new loop is ordinary language in, decision-ready shortlist out:

    SEARCH → READ → VERIFY

    Search does not dump two hundred hits. It returns a small set of routes that are actually usable: an , a person profile, a strategy record, a page, a current working set. Reading then opens bounded context — enough to judge the result, not so much that the model or the user drowns in neighboring files. Verify is not optional decoration. Before a metric, role, story, or commitment moves into a or , the governing reopens. Page, section, or row locator when one exists.

    That loop is the product surface. Everything else in the system exists to make those three steps honest.

    It also replaced a social habit. Instead of “who should I call,” the first move became “what does the library already know, and what does the actually say.” The phone call is still available. It is no longer the catalog.

    2. Storage is not authority

    Most knowledge bases collapse the moment they generate something useful. A registry looks official. A search card looks like a fact. An AI packet looks like a brief. People stop opening the original.

    The folder structure refuses that collapse. It keeps different kinds of files in different kinds of rooms:

    • Intake is not knowledge yet. It is material waiting to be classified and checked.
    • preserves converted and the locator back to it. This is where a claim can be proven or killed.
    • Reusable knowledge holds the controlled objects: people, projects, stories, strategy, relationships, responses. These are curated. They are still not the original.
    • work holds compact intelligence for a live chase. The actual being written lives in a sibling workspace on purpose. Historical research and live production do not share a drawer.

    That split is the difference between a library and a pile. A generated view can help you navigate. It cannot become the of record just because it is cleaner than the PDF it came from. If the original is missing and only a converted derivative remains, that limitation stays attached to the record. The system would rather look incomplete than look more certain than the .

    3. Evidence-aware retrieval

    Keyword search treats every match as the same kind of object. “Smith” in a cover letter, “Smith” on a , and “Smith” in a narrative about who ran night work are not the same finding. A marketing system that cannot tell those apart will eventually write the wrong sentence.

    Retrieval here is layered on purpose:

    • a relationship mesh so questions can follow people to projects to to stories
    • bounded full-text indexes so a hit has an edge instead of swallowing the whole corpus
    • source-aware markdown records so a result still knows which file and which locator it came from
    • a semantic-search pilot so phrasing can vary without requiring the exact title someone used three years ago

    Questions get routed by type. A project question, a people question, an question, a relationship question, and a decision question are not the same query with different keywords. They travel different paths and return different kinds of objects.

    When AI enters the loop, it does not get the whole disk. It gets a context packet: the narrowed question, selected excerpts, structured routes, paths, page or row locators when recorded, status, review limits, and omissions. The packet is also allowed to say what is missing. Private text stays on the machine. The workflow does not depend on uploading the corpus to a cloud search service.

    The point of retrieval is not “answer.” It is “here is the shortlist, here is the , here is what you still do not know.”

    That behavior was earned, not assumed. Early search work produced raw JSON and implementation dumps — paths, scores, internals. That is not what a marketer needs at 4 p.m. on a . The instruction was to answer the way a person would ask: five normal questions, five usable answers.

    One of those checks was simple on purpose: What is the for [anonymized federal project]? The system returned Design-Build, first marked imported-unverified. The original workbook row was then opened. The actually recorded Design-Build. The point was not the two-word answer. The point was that an imported label is not finished work until the row is reopened.

    The same pass exposed the usual RAG failure modes: duplicate story results, unrelated passages stuck to a narrow question, and weak partial matches for a project that did not exist. Six retrieval changes went in after that:

    1. Give the model a focused packet, not the vault.
    2. Keep passages with context, citations, and limits.
    3. Combine keyword search with meaning-based search.
    4. Rank relevant and collapse duplicates.
    5. Separate facts, experience, appearances, and multipart questions.
    6. Return “” instead of filling gaps with weak matches.

    Live checks after the change confirmed the intended behavior: an invented project could return insufficient , duplicate stories could collapse into one result, and differently worded questions could still find the relevant example.

    On a live county public-safety CMAR pursuit, the question was not a project name. It was a problem: Find a prior example addressing schedule management, submittals, coordination, critical-path risk, and material availability.

    The problem-first search checked 64 story files and 87 matching passages, then returned three focused candidates:

    • a police-facility example involving open-book cost control, coordination, and a qualitative on-schedule result
    • a public-safety example involving temporary power, material protection, and supplier coordination, flagged for original-page verification
    • a public-library example involving temporary plant preservation, surfaced as a weaker topical match that still needed validation

    A folder search would have returned filenames and raw matches across project pages, conversions, notes, and other records. The useful difference was that the shortlist grouped by the actual problem, explained relevance, and showed review status. It did not treat three search hits as three cleared stories.

    4. Quality control is the product

    This is the design decision that matters most, and the one a generic RAG demo always skips.

    marketing copy fails in predictable ways. Someone appeared in a , so they “led the job.” A project exists on a , so it is “our story.” Two names share a project page, so they “worked together.” A sentence sounds like a result, so it becomes a metric. A file was touched yesterday, so it is the current draft.

    The system makes those errors expensive by keeping separate routes:

    Reported work vs. a appearance. Being named in a document is not that a person performed the work. The people route can show that someone was listed. It does not automatically promote that listing into a performed-role claim.

    vs. project description or . There are 8,705 canonical project views and 9 . That ratio is the point. A can be a lead. It is not cleared narrative. If you want the strongest example of , you do not get every building that ever had a campus address. You get the reviewed shortlist, with pages, status, and reuse limits.

    Proposed team roster vs. verified collaboration. A names a team because that is how are built. Naming two people on the same page does not prove they have run a job together. Collaboration is its own observation, with its own boundary.

    fact vs. inference, recommendation, or unresolved hypothesis. “The document says X” and “therefore we should claim Y” are different objects. The second one does not get to wear the first one’s certainty.

    Current working draft vs. recently edited file. Modification date is a bad proxy for authority. The current-work route identifies the controlling working set for a . Newest file is not the same thing as governing file.

    These distinctions are not metadata garnish. They are the reason the system can be used on a live without quietly upgrading a convenient hit into a stronger claim than the supports.

    That rule has stopped real draft language. A prior public-safety options study looked like a clean case-study metric because a stated approximately $580,000 in savings. Verification traced the number to a converted extract rather than a locally available original page. The record also did not provide an original or final GMP, and a related repository heading had a project-label mismatch.

    That was enough. The draft was not allowed to say “we saved $580,000 on the project,” add days recovered, claim acceptance, or assign the result to an individual. The narrow source-stated figure stayed internal-review language until the original page could be checked.

    The verification loop is the same idea in motion: read the , write the draft, compare the draft back against the , fix anything unsupported or overstated. An overall “supported” judgment is not enough. It can pass while a factual passage never gets compared. The tightened rule requires complete draft coverage, exact supporting quotations, facts separated from recommendations and transitions, and review tied to the exact saved version.

    A search hit also does not become an by itself. A human reviewer opens the consolidated past or SOQ and verifies the original pages, identity, project identity, and condition / action / result narrative. The reviewer records exact pages, route, attribution, type, recommended use, and limits in the story manifest. A validator checks the transcript and page boundaries before the story is published to the story shelf and the retrieval index.

    When that story is needed on a new , the pursuit owner still selects it and performs claim-specific review against the current requirement. “Cleared for reuse” means the narrative is source-reviewed and eligible to be surfaced as a bounded reuse candidate. It does not mean current staffing is confirmed, the accepted the result, the outcome was independently audited, or the story can be copied into any without fresh review.

    5. Strategy is preserved, not just documents

    A archive answers “what did we send.” That is only half of the useful question. The other half is “what was the asking, how did we choose to answer, what did we put under that answer, and what did we refuse to over-claim.”

    The records exist for that second half. Across 137 of them, the library keeps the shape of a past response:

    • the requirement, as asked
    • the response structure the firm used
    • the chosen to support it
    • the documented intent of that answer
    • the limits that still apply if someone wants to reuse it

    That makes old work useful as a pattern instead of as a sacred text. You can see how a similar question was handled without pretending the old answer caused the win. A folder labeled “award” is not causation. A loss is not proof the story was wrong. Strategy records are allowed to be reused only as adaptation material, with the limits still attached.

    For a marketer, this is the difference between rewriting from a blank page and starting from a reviewed argument that already survived an scoring matrix once.

    This feature exists because a summary was not enough. Processing an or had to record, question by question: what the asked; how the firm answered; what or example was selected; what strategy was documented versus inferred; and what might be useful on a future .

    That is why a later question — could I ask what our strategy or positioning was for this , this client type, or this question? — has a direct answer. Yes. That is what the feature is for. It returns a small set of precedents: the ask, the firm’s answer, how the response was structured, what reasoning was actually documented, and what has to change before reuse. Inferred strategy is labeled as inferred. Documented strategy is labeled as documented. Those are not the same claim.

    6. People, projects, and relationships are canonical

    Without canonical routes, every invents its own universe. The same person gets three spellings. The same job gets two names and a third nickname the uses on the phone. The relationship lives in someone’s head. Marketing spends the first hour of a reconciling identity instead of choosing a story.

    The library maintains one route per controlled person and one route per canonical project, then hangs the rest of the graph on those routes:

    • links (this person or project appeared in this response)
    • worked-on links (reported participation, still distinct from a story)
    • attributed-project links
    • and client connections
    • team contexts
    • typed relationships
    • direct collaboration observations

    Generated views exist so a human can walk that graph without holding it in memory. They do not get to erase the boundary underneath. You can start at a person and get to the projects they are linked to. You can start at a project and see which stories are , which connections are typed, and which collaborations are actually observed. You cannot start at a search snippet and treat it as the person record.

    This is also how 317 people profiles and 8,705 project views stay usable. Volume is not the achievement. One identity per person and one identity per project is the achievement. Volume is what that decision has to survive.

    7. Human-and-AI memory with a review gate

    Useful knowledge shows up in conversation: a correction from a , a warning that a proposed approach is not verified past performance, a note that two people have actually run work together, a question that the library cannot answer yet. If those insights stay in chat logs, they die. If they get written straight into the people or project record, they become fake facts.

    The contribution layer is the compromise. Capture the smallest reusable object — an insight, a correction, a question, a decision signal, a team context, a relationship — and mark it review-required. The conversation is provenance for the capture. It is not proof of a role, a metric, an outcome, a collaboration, or a commitment.

    Promotion to reusable knowledge takes a person. That rule is what lets AI sit in the workflow at all. The model can help draft a packet, surface a candidate link, or notice a contradiction. It cannot author the record of record. A marketer under deadline will always be tempted to let a fluent paragraph become the . The review gate is there because I have felt that temptation.

    The other half of the rule is restraint about volume. Do not save the whole transcript. Save the sentence that would still matter next month.

    8. Maintenance that makes gaps visible

    A knowledge base that only looks good on the day you build it is a demo. work happens on weeks when files are missing, names conflict, and someone converted a PDF six months ago and the original left the machine.

    So the system spends real effort on being inspectable:

    • intake checkpoints, so new material cannot skip classification
    • source-availability records, so “we have a derivative but not the original” is a visible state
    • link checks, so a relationship that points nowhere does not sit quietly
    • generated-view validators, so a pretty index cannot drift from the records it claims to represent
    • retrieval freshness checks, so yesterday’s index is not treated as today’s corpus
    • health reports, so “is this safe to rely on” is an actual question with an actual answer
    • review queues, so pending human judgment has a place to live
    • golden retrieval tests, so a known question still returns the known good route

    A dated health check on this library came back Attention, not Blocked. Retrieval, navigation, maintained links, and the canonical registry were ready. availability and people-quality review still had open items. That is the kind of status a serious system should be willing to show. Ready-on-one-control is not a full audit. Converted-only still has a hash and an identity; it is still not the original file.

    The standard is not “no gaps.” The standard is “gaps cannot hide behind a clean search result.”

    What IT inherits — and what stays local

    The national build is not a copy of the vault. It is a copy of the rules.

    IT inherits the behavior contract, schemas, validators, sanitized instructions, and redacted or synthetic fixtures needed to reproduce the workflow. The proof of concept does not hand over the live vault, raw and , , references, pricing, schedules, active drafts, response-evidence sidecars, complete sessions, email, chat history, credentials, contact details, or private paths.

    The later system can change the model, the storage layer, and the interface. It still has to preserve provenance, bounded retrieval, visible uncertainty, human story-selection gates, external-transmission controls, and independently reviewable outputs. Raw material remains unless separately approved.

    That split is part of why the work was adoptable. A marketing POC that requires IT to absorb every confidential file dies in review. A POC that can be rebuilt from behavior and fixtures can travel.

    How it was used

    Primary use was marketing work: research for and . When another person needed an ultra-specific, tightly sourced answer, they came through that workflow rather than browsing the file tree themselves.

    Practical questions the system is built to answer:

    What are the current drafts for this — not merely the newest file?

    What is our strongest reviewed example for a construction problem (, , a delivery constraint)?

    Who has reported experience here, without collapsing a appearance into performed work?

    How did we answer a similar question, including choice and reuse limits?

    Where does this metric, role, result, or requirement live in the governing ?

    Can I hand another person or an AI a bounded context packet with excerpts, locators, status, constraints, and instructions to flag missing instead of inventing it?

    Is the knowledge base healthy enough to rely on today?

    Before: story hunting meant calling the field and hoping.

    After: a normal-language question, a shortlist, and a path back to the page.

    Time dropped from an unreliable phone tree to a few minutes. The more important change was trust. Research could move faster and reopen the before a claim entered a , , or briefing.

    Judgment rules

    Most marketing systems store files. This one stores judgment rules.

    It made the firm’s best writing reusable without turning institutional memory into folklore. It gave marketing a front door that felt like asking a colleague, then forced the colleague to show their work. It let AI operate on local context packets — excerpts, paths, page or row locators, status, review limits, omissions — without treating the model as an author of record.

    It also stayed honest about what it does not prove:

    • A search result identifies a place to read. It does not prove the claim.
    • Appearing in a does not mean a person performed every example project.
    • A , , or project page is not automatically a .
    • Sharing a project does not prove two people collaborated.
    • A generated index, relationship edge, or AI packet is not independent .
    • A win/loss label does not explain why the was won or lost.
    • A passing health check on one control is not a full audit.

    The working system on a private was enough proof of value that IT is now building it out for national rollout. I created the model of record: the zones, the routes, the rules, the people/project/story/strategy graph, and the SEARCH → READ → VERIFY loop the larger build has to honor.
