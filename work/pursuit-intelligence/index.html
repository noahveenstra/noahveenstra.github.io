Pursuit Intelligence

A local evidence library for AEC proposal and interview work

Role: concept, data model, ingestion, retrieval, evidence rules

Scope: 2.5 years of proposals · marketing team · national IT build-out in progress

This is the most consequential thing I have built in my career. Not because it is a flashy model, but because it changed how a marketing team finds, verifies, and reuses the only content that actually wins work: project stories, people evidence, and past response strategy.

One-liner: I designed and built a local-first knowledge system that turned years of proposals, source documents, people records, and pursuit decisions into a searchable, evidence-traceable decision library — then proved it hard enough that IT is now building it out for national rollout.

Role: Marketing professional. I owned the problem, the information architecture, the ingestion, the retrieval design, the quality rules, and the operating loop. I did not hand a brief to engineering and wait. I built the working system the team used.

If you are not in AEC

Construction firms do not usually sell from a catalog. They compete for individual jobs. An owner (the client) issues a request for qualifications or a proposal. The contractor’s marketing team writes a response that argues: we have done this kind of work, with this kind of team, in this kind of constraint. The evidence that actually lands is not a slogan. It is a project story — a specific account of what happened on a real job.

That is the industry context for this system. The rest of the page uses the terms below.

Glossary

AEC
Architecture, engineering, and construction. The industry this work sits in. The firm here is a contractor competing for building projects.
Owner
The client. A school district, hospital system, city, developer, or other organization paying for the building. In a proposal, “how we worked with the owner” means how the contractor communicated, solved problems, and protected the client’s operations while construction was underway.
Architect
The design firm. Contractors and architects have to coordinate constantly. A strong proposal often has to show that this contractor has already worked well with a given architect or design approach — not just that it can pour concrete.
Pursuit
A live chase for a specific job. From the first owner document through the written proposal and, if you advance, the interview. “Pursuit work” is the intelligence and writing tied to that chase.
Proposal
The formal written response. It is part marketing document, part evidence file: team résumés, project examples, schedules, safety, and answers to the owner’s exact questions.
RFQ / RFP
Request for Qualifications / Request for Proposals. The owner’s packet that says what they want and how they will score responses. An “RFQ-to-response record” in this system is: what they asked, how the firm answered, what evidence was used, and what limits still apply.
Interview
The shortlist meeting after the written proposal. Marketing and operations prepare talking points, project examples, and team chemistry. Bad or unverified stories are expensive here.
Project story
The reusable narrative of a real job: the constraint, what the team did in the field, how they worked with the owner and architect, how trades were coordinated, and what that proves for the next pursuit. This is the content that carries weight. A project name on a slide is not a story.
Authorized story
A project story that has been reviewed and cleared for reuse. In this system, thousands of projects can exist as records. Only a small set is treated as an authorized story. That gap is intentional.
Project sheet / résumé row
A short listing of a job on a person’s résumé or a one-page project cut sheet. Useful as a lead. Not automatically a story, and not proof that the person performed every task listed nearby.
Project manager (PM)
The contractor’s lead for running the job — schedule, budget, owner communication, trade coordination. Historically the person marketing called when they needed a story.
Superintendent
The contractor’s lead on the jobsite. Closest to what actually happened in the field. Also historically on the other end of that phone call.
Subcontractor / trade
Specialist firms the contractor hires (electrical, mechanical, concrete, and so on). “How we coordinated other trades” is often the difference between a generic capability claim and a believable field story.
Business unit
An internal division of the firm (by region, market, or type of work). Stories, people, and past pursuits belong to units. Connecting those links is part of what the system does.
Preconstruction
Work before groundbreaking: estimating, scheduling, constructability review, phasing, and helping the owner and architect make the job buildable. “Public-safety preconstruction” means that kind of planning on a police, fire, or similar facility.
Occupied-campus phasing
Building or renovating while the place stays open — a school in session, a hospital treating patients. High-stakes storytelling because the owner cares as much about not disrupting operations as about the finished building.
Delivery method
How the contract is structured (design-bid-build, CM at risk, design-build, and so on). Past response strategy often hinges on matching the owner’s delivery method, not just the building type.
Evidence / source
The governing document a claim came from: a page in an old proposal, an owner RFQ, a project fact sheet. “Verify” means reopen that page before the claim is reused.
Local-first / on disk
The corpus stays on a local machine. Sensitive proposal, people, and project material is not uploaded to a cloud search product as the default workflow.

The problem

In AEC marketing, the gold is the project story. What the company actually did in the field. How it worked with the owner. How it worked with the architect. How it coordinated other trades. Those stories carry a proposal. Capability slides do not.

The firm had been writing those stories for years. It had not cataloged them. There was no reliable place to ask a specific question and get a specific, sourced answer. The shared catalog that existed was incomplete and never sharp enough from pursuit to pursuit.

The real workflow was a phone call. Marketing called the project manager or superintendent, hoped they picked up, and often got pointed to another proposal someone had already been told to write. That was slow, uneven, and easy to treat as fact when it was only memory.

The hidden risk was worse than slowness. Once a team starts “searching” old files with AI, it becomes easy to repeat an unsupported claim: a person who merely appeared in a proposal becomes “the PM on that job,” a resume row becomes a project story, a shared project becomes proven collaboration, a win label becomes a causal theory. I needed something that made proposal work faster without making bad claims easier to recycle.

What I built

I built a local-first evidence and retrieval system on disk — markdown source material, structured records, and a relationship layer — organized so marketing could work in a simple loop:

SEARCH → READ → VERIFY

Search returns a small, decision-ready shortlist. Reading opens bounded context. Verification reopens the governing source before a fact, metric, role, story, or commitment is reused.

The system is organized around four zones:

ZoneJob
IntakeNew material sits here until it is classified and checked
EvidenceConverted source material and locators so a claim can be reopened
Reusable knowledgeControlled people, project, story, strategy, relationship, and response records
Pursuit workCompact pursuit intelligence and interview material; live production stays in the proposal workspace

It is not a task tracker. It is not the live proposal file. It is not permission to treat a search hit as client-ready copy. It is a decision library with boundaries.

I ingested 2.5 years of proposals and the surrounding source material into that structure. As of a dated internal snapshot:

  • 1,354 files in intake
  • 38,297 evidence files, including 37,928 markdown derivatives
  • 317 controlled people profiles
  • 8,705 canonical project views
  • 9 authorized project stories (on purpose — a story is a reviewed object, not every project page)
  • 140 pursuit workspaces
  • 137 RFQ-to-response strategy records

Those numbers matter less than the distinction they encode. The system can hold thousands of project views and still refuse to call something an authorized story until it earns that status.

What made it more than a search box

1. A usable front door

Folder-browsing stopped being the primary experience. The team asks a plain-language question and gets a shortlist instead of a pile of PDFs.

2. Storage is not authority

Original evidence, reusable knowledge, intake/health controls, historical pursuit intelligence, and live proposal production are separate. A generated registry, search card, or AI context packet cannot quietly pose as an independent source.

3. Evidence-aware retrieval

A structured relationship mesh, bounded full-text indexes, source-aware markdown records, and a local semantic-search pilot route questions about projects, people, proposals, authorized stories, relationships, and decisions. Keyword matches are not treated as equal. Private text stays local. The workflow does not require uploading the corpus to a cloud search service.

4. Quality control is the product

The system keeps separate routes for:

  • reported work vs. a person merely appearing in a proposal
  • an authorized project story vs. a project description or resume row
  • a proposed team roster vs. verified direct collaboration
  • a source fact vs. an inference, recommendation, or unresolved hypothesis
  • a current working draft vs. a file that was only edited recently

Those distinctions are the main quality control. They stop a convenient result from becoming a stronger claim than the source supports.

5. Strategy is preserved, not just documents

Historical RFQ-to-response records capture what the owner asked, how the firm answered, what evidence was used, what intent was documented, and what limits remain. Past work becomes a response pattern. A win, a loss, or a folder label is not treated as proof of causation.

6. People, projects, and relationships are canonical

Controlled profiles and routes cover proposal-use links, worked-on links, attributed projects, architect and client connections, authorized stories, team contexts, typed relationships, and direct collaboration observations. Generated views make the graph navigable without erasing evidence boundaries.

7. Human-and-AI memory with a review gate

Insights, corrections, questions, decision signals, and relationships can be captured as small review-required records. A conversation is provenance for the capture — not automatic proof of a role, metric, outcome, collaboration, or commitment. Nothing promotes to fact without a person.

8. Maintenance that makes gaps visible

Intake checkpoints, source-availability records, link checks, generated-view validators, retrieval freshness checks, health reports, review queues, and golden retrieval tests. A clean search result is not allowed to hide missing originals, unresolved names, or source conflicts.

How people actually used it

The marketing team was the primary user. Anyone else who needed an ultra-specific story or a tightly sourced answer came to that team.

Practical questions the system is built to answer:

  • What are the current drafts for this pursuit — not merely the newest file?
  • What is our strongest reviewed example for a construction problem (occupied-campus phasing, public-safety preconstruction, a delivery constraint)?
  • Who has reported experience here, without collapsing a proposal appearance into performed work?
  • How did we answer a similar RFQ question, including evidence choice and reuse limits?
  • Where does this metric, role, result, or owner requirement live in the governing source?
  • Can I hand another person or an AI a bounded context packet with excerpts, locators, status, constraints, and instructions to flag missing evidence instead of inventing it?
  • Is the knowledge base healthy enough to rely on today?

Before: story hunting meant calling the field and hoping.

After: a normal-language question, a shortlist, and a path back to the page.

Time dropped from an unreliable phone tree to a few minutes. The more important change was trust. The team could move faster and reopen the source before a claim entered a proposal, interview, or briefing.

Why this was a career-defining piece of work

Most marketing systems store files. This one stores judgment rules.

It made the firm’s best writing reusable without turning institutional memory into folklore. It gave marketing a front door that felt like asking a colleague, then forced the colleague to show their work. It let AI operate on local context packets — excerpts, paths, page or row locators, evidence status, review limits, omissions — without treating the model as an author of record.

It also stayed honest about what it does not prove:

  • A search result identifies a place to read. It does not prove the claim.
  • Appearing in a proposal does not mean a person performed every example project.
  • A resume, project sheet, or project page is not automatically a project story.
  • Sharing a project does not prove two people collaborated.
  • A generated index, relationship edge, or AI packet is not independent evidence.
  • A win/loss label does not explain why the pursuit was won or lost.
  • A passing health check on one control is not a full source audit.

That restraint is why the system was credible enough to grow. A POC that only “finds stuff” gets demoed. A POC that refuses to over-claim gets adopted.

The working system on my disk was enough proof of value that IT is now building it out for national rollout. I created the model of record: the zones, the routes, the evidence rules, the people/project/story/strategy graph, and the SEARCH → READ → VERIFY loop the larger build has to honor.

Impact

  • Scope: 2.5 years of proposals and source material, tens of thousands of evidence files, hundreds of people profiles, thousands of project views, 140 pursuit workspaces
  • Users: marketing first; the rest of the business when the question had to be exact
  • Speed: story research from an unreliable field call to minutes
  • Quality: authorized stories and verified collaboration kept separate from convenient lookalikes
  • Confidentiality: sensitive proposal, people, and project material stayed local
  • Outcome: internal system now being productized by IT for national use

I am a marketing professional. I built the intelligence layer that connected the firm’s proposals, people, projects, and pursuit strategy into something the team could actually use — and I designed it so speed never outran the source.
