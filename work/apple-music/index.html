Apple Music

Concept · Product design · 2022

Overview

I use the rest of the Apple stack, and I still kept ending up on Spotify. So did almost everyone around me. Eight of ten listeners in that circle used Spotify on Apple hardware.

A 2018 Statista cut put only 17 percent of Apple Music’s 49.5 million US users in the 18 to 24 range, about 8.4 million people. Spotify’s share of that same group was 26 percent, about 46.8 million. This case study is about why that happens, and what Apple could do about it.

The problem

Apple Music launched in 2015 with Connect, a channel between artists and listeners. It was gone a year later. Since then, features have more often been removed than added. Spatial audio and lossless arrived, and both need the right hardware and the right conditions.

Spotify kept shipping: collaborative playlists, Blend, DJ, Wrapped, podcasts, social listening. The product feels alive. Apple Music feels like it is waiting for someone to care.

Research

I surveyed friends who use both services, on a Jamboard, for a week. What each app does well, where each fails, and what would actually change a switching decision.

Sorted by priority and how hard each idea would be, two kept coming up: a place to see everything you have loved, and a way to listen together without a workaround.

Loves

Apple Music lets you love a song, an album, an artist, or a playlist. Then it disappears into the algorithm. For some people that missing shelf is reason enough to leave.

The proposal is a Loves tab in the bottom bar, in place of Radio, which moves into Browse. On Rotation is a playlist of the songs you return to. Recently Loved is a chronological carousel of songs, albums, playlists, and artists. Loved by Mood sorts by energy, using BPM, and mixes in similar recommendations so discovery stays inside taste you already trust.

SharePlay

Sharing a speaker between iPhones still means someone disconnects Bluetooth and someone else reconnects. SharePlay exists, but it requires a FaceTime call, and that is enough friction that people do not use it.

The proposal puts SharePlay in Apple Music itself. Up to 15 people on one queue, no video call. The host controls playback and order. Guests can add songs. They cannot skip or reorder. Every entry shows who added it, and the host can remove someone if the queue goes sideways.
